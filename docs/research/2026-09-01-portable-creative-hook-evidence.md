# Portable Creative Hook Evidence — 2026-09-01

## Purpose

This note records the evidence used to seed Hottop's product-independent creative logic taxonomy. It separates official platform guidance, published research, project-local performance observations, and working hypotheses.

The goal is **not** to claim a universal formula for virality. The goal is to establish useful priors that can be tested against fresh hotspot evidence and actual platform performance.

## Evidence classes

### A. Platform official guidance

#### TikTok / short-form video

TikTok for Business recommends a `hook -> body -> close` structure, stresses that much of ad recall impact is captured in the opening seconds, and explicitly names suspense, surprise and emotion as useful hook mechanisms. It also recommends platform-native creative, text overlays/captions, trends as storytelling templates, and early communication of value.

Sources:

- TikTok for Business, “Creative Best Practices for TikTok Ads”: https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads
- TikTok Ads Help, “Creative best practices for performance ads” (updated June 2025): https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices
- TikTok for Business, short-video best practice: https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-GB/blog/tiktok-short-video-best-practice

Portable lessons:

- the first beat must earn attention before exposition;
- question, suspense, surprise, emotion and visible movement are legitimate hook families;
- platform-native language/format matters;
- a trend is better treated as a storytelling template/mechanism than a logo-placement opportunity.

#### YouTube

YouTube Help distinguishes searchable and intriguing titles, recommends accurate and succinct titles with important words early, and emphasizes that title + thumbnail together set expectations. It recommends simple readable thumbnails and evaluating packaging with analytics rather than assuming one style always works.

Sources:

- YouTube Help, “Thumbnail & title tips”: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300
- YouTube Help, “Understand your content performance for YouTube’s recommendation system”: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650

Portable lessons:

- curiosity must remain accurate;
- title/thumbnail packaging is part of the product, not metadata decoration;
- different audiences and discovery surfaces require different title strategies;
- analytics should update priors.

### B. Published research

#### Curiosity / information gaps

Research on curiosity describes it as motivation created by a gap between what people know and what they want to know. A 2026 advertising study also found curiosity mediated engagement with AI-involved advertising under its tested conditions.

Sources:

- “Deprivation and discovery motives determine how it feels to be curious,” Current Opinion in Behavioral Sciences (2020): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352154620301194
- “How AI involvement affects curiosity and content engagement in AI-generated advertisements,” Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services (2026): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0969698926000366

Portable lesson:

- `到底/为什么/值多少/谁来解释` hooks work best when there is a real, concrete information gap and the content actually closes it.

#### Sharing, emotion, surprise and practical value

Berger & Milkman's work found that sharing is associated with high-arousal emotion, and that surprising, interesting and practically useful content were positively associated with virality in their dataset. This does **not** mean “make everything angry” or “shock at any cost”; it supports emotional activation and useful/surprising content as candidate mechanisms.

Sources:

- Berger & Milkman, “What Makes Online Content Viral?”, Journal of Marketing Research (2012): https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.10.0353
- Knowledge at Wharton summary of Berger's STEPPS framework: https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/contagious-jonah-berger-on-why-things-catch-on/

Portable lessons:

- `emotional_reaction`, `practical_value`, `unexpected_reversal` and `social_proof/trigger` are legitimate families;
- high arousal is not the same as negativity;
- practical value can make content worth saving/sharing.

#### Indirect/mystery advertising

Research comparing direct and indirect advertising found indirect executions can require more elaboration and, under tested conditions, improve originality/surprise and some brand-memory/preference outcomes. Earlier mystery-ad research also found delayed brand reveal can strengthen category-brand associations for novel brands.

Sources:

- Simola et al., “Attention, memory and preference for direct and indirect print advertisements,” Journal of Business Research (2020): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296319303881
- “On the Development and Strength of Category–Brand Associations in Memory: The Case of Mystery Ads,” Journal of Consumer Psychology (1992): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1057740808800423

Portable lesson:

- a meme/event can earn attention before the product reveal, but the product must still own the causal resolution;
- mystery cannot become ambiguity: the payoff must connect clearly to the product.

#### Surprise

A 2025 Journal of Retailing review frames surprise marketing around unexpected/uncertain elements that can evoke strong positive emotion, while emphasizing boundary conditions.

Source:

- “Understanding surprise: Toward a theory of surprise marketing,” Journal of Retailing (2025): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022435924000691

Portable lesson:

- surprise is strongest when it changes an expectation coherently; random weirdness is not a strategy.

#### Taboo / shock

An eye-tracking advertising study found taboo content affected attention and recall under its conditions, but effects are mixed in the broader literature and high complexity can reduce the effect.

Source:

- “An eye-tracking study of attention to brand-identifying content and recall of taboo advertising,” Journal of Business Research (2020): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0148296319304795

Portable lesson:

- `taboo_double_meaning` is a high-risk optional family, never a default. Use only when brand/platform safety permits and resolve quickly into safe meaning.

### C. Project-local account performance evidence

Source: user-provided screenshots from Douyin, Xiaohongshu, WeChat Channels and Kuaishou on 2026-09-01.

Observed patterns for this account/content history:

- concrete conflict/outcome framing has often outperformed product-name-first self-introduction;
- “不用部署” / direct benefit language performed better than generic “who still doesn't know [brand]” framing in the shown Douyin history;
- event/number hooks such as NASA / large-number conflict earned meaningful recommendation traffic or stronger relative views in the shown examples;
- Xiaohongshu examples suggest useful/analysis-oriented content can outperform pure launch/self-introduction posts;
- low average image depth in at least one shown image post supports putting the core value/joke in the first image.

Classification: `account_specific_observation`.

Do not generalize these results into platform laws. They should influence ranking for this project until contradicted by newer comparable data.

### D. 2026-09-01 “Unitree valuation question” lesson

Fresh Eastmoney discussion showed the current popular question `市值蒸发2000亿，宇树科技到底值多少？` as a top community topic. The user preferred the creative bridge:

`大家都想知道宇树值多少钱，问 [product]`

The portable learning is **not** “always use Unitree” and **not** “always use InkClawAgent.”

Generalized mechanism:

`active public question -> product performs the real work required to investigate/answer/act`

Possible product-role migration:

- research/finance tool -> source gathering, assumptions, valuation memo;
- sales radar -> identify real buyers/sellers/demand signals behind a hot market;
- knowledge tool -> organize sources and decision context;
- automation/agent tool -> execute the workflow triggered by the question;
- creative tool -> turn the question into a publishable visual/video;
- coding tool -> implement/test the technical task implied by the event.

Guardrail: in finance, do not invent a valuation conclusion or imply personalized investment advice.

## Working hypotheses to test, not facts

The library contains many useful hook families whose effectiveness depends on audience, category and platform:

- specific numbers;
- before/after;
- role reversal;
- contradiction/paradox;
- identity callouts;
- myth busting;
- challenge/test;
- hidden causal layer;
- one-sentence compression;
- constraint deletion;
- pain-to-prop visual metaphor.

They are **candidate mechanisms**, not guaranteed winners.

Each new campaign should record:

- selected logic IDs;
- hotspot evidence;
- product role;
- platform;
- title/copy;
- asset format;
- performance metrics when available;
- user qualitative feedback.

That creates a path from broad priors to project-specific learning.
